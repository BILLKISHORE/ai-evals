title: "EU AI Act Articles 55 & 73"
description: "Map findings to specific EU AI Act obligations"
---

The `eu-ai-act` scorecard maps your runs onto specific obligations of
[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/): Article 55
(obligations for general-purpose AI models with systemic risk, including
adversarial testing) and Article 73 (serious-incident reporting).

This is finer-grained than the `compliance` scorecard, which shows EU AI Act
risk tiers. Here each obligation is a row, and the scorecard says which of your
runs speak to it.

## Running it

```bash
# Populate the database first
ai-blackteam batch -p anthropic -m claude-sonnet-4-6 -t "harmful request"

# Score against the article obligations
ai-blackteam scorecard --standard eu-ai-act

# JSON export
ai-blackteam scorecard --standard eu-ai-act --format json -o eu-ai-act.json
```

## Reading the table

Each obligation carries its own status and counts:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `EVIDENCED` | Runs produced something that speaks to this obligation |
| `PARTIAL` | Some of what the obligation asks for |
| `NOT_EVIDENCED` | In reach, but no run covered it |
| `READINESS_ONLY` | Results help a later response; not compliance evidence now |
| `OUT_OF_SCOPE` | No adversarial run can speak to it, with a reason |

Runs that errored are counted separately and never reported as bypasses. Five
provider timeouts against a systemic-risk category show up as five **errored**
runs, not five systemic-risk bypasses. A control that could not run is not a
finding.

<Note>
The obligation identifiers (55(1)(a) through 73(6)) are real provisions of the
regulation, but the mapping from ai-blackteam findings to obligations is this
project's interpretation, marked unverified in the data. It is a starting point
for an audit conversation, not a legal compliance certificate.
</Note>
